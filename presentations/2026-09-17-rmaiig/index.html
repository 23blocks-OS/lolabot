What Lola is responsible for

For me

  • My inbox — reads it, drafts, sends, replies as me
  • My memory — customers, decisions, health, family
  • My tasks — what is due, what is stuck, what I forgot
  • My documents — filed, indexed, findable

For the companies

  • Four companies, four general managers
  • She talks to the four. Never to their crews.
  • Owns the shared brain — standards, policies, roles
  • Decides what actually reaches me

She does not do the work. She knows everything and hands it to whoever should have it.

TIP 01

Give it a brain
it writes to.

Not a prompt file that grows until nobody reads it. A folder the agent updates — customers, decisions, people.

A brain you maintain by hand is a document.
A brain it maintains is a memory.

TIP 02

Never believe
"I found nothing."

> check every file for the old API key Searched 143 files. No matches. ✓ clean

The search was filtered to *.js. Half the codebase is .ts.

A measurement that fails for the wrong reason looks exactly like a result.

TIP 03

Talk to one agent.
Not five.

One that knows your business and hands work to the others. You stop being the router between your own tools.

> the website needs the new pricing Sent to the GM. Its web agent has it. I don't write the copy — I know who does.
Slide 8

Conclusions

The agent is not the hard part. Continuity is. An agent that does everything and remembers nothing makes you the memory.

Could a new session, with no history, do what it just did?
If yes, you have not built anything yet.

Bonus track

Take care of your
mental health.

AI agents are a massive cognitive load for us — humble humans.

They never sleep. They always answer. They generate more in an hour than you can read in a day, and every one of them is waiting for you.

Decide when you are off. The agents will still be there.
That is the whole point of having them.
